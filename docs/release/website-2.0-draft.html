Quick Fix

Select it. Fix it. See what changed.

Select text in almost any app and press the Quick Fix shortcut [default: confirm]. KeyReed corrects typos, missing apostrophes, doubled words and common slips, and shows every changed word in a small review panel before anything is replaced. Enter replaces, Esc leaves your text alone. [confirm before publishing]

It fixes; it does not rewrite. Links, e-mail addresses, file paths, numbers, names, code and shell commands are left exactly as you typed them. After a fix, Undo stays on the pill for a few seconds. English only for now. [confirm before publishing]

The Composer

A small writing window, beside your text.

Click the KeyReed pill. If you had text selected, a chip says exactly what will be used ("Using selection, 412 characters from Outlook") and you can remove it. Choose Fix, Improve, Shorter, Change tone, Summarise, or type your own instruction. The answer streams in as it is written; Esc stops it. Then Insert, Replace, Copy or Regenerate. [confirm before publishing]

The window always says where the work runs: "On this PC, model name" or "Cloud, host, model". Nothing is sent anywhere before you press Submit. Cloud is its own switch, off by default, and agreeing to cloud dictation does not send a selected email. [confirm before publishing]

Your model, your choice

Start small. Go bigger if your PC can.

The Composer works with the small model KeyReed already ships. For better writing you can download Qwen3.5 2B (about 1.3 GB) or 4B (about 2.7 GB), both Apache-2.0, and KeyReed suggests one that fits your PC's memory and processor. Only one is loaded at a time, and every file is verified before it is used. [confirm before publishing]

Speak your instruction

Say what you want done to the selection.

The dictation key works inside the Composer. A separate shortcut [confirm it shipped] takes a spoken instruction ("make this shorter and friendlier") and applies it to your selection, with the result shown for review. [confirm before publishing]

My style

It learns your words, and shows you what it learned.

Correct "cooper netties" to "Kubernetes" once and KeyReed notices. The second time, the word goes into your dictionary, which also helps the speech recogniser, and the pill says so once, with Undo. A correction seen once waits in a Suggestions list. [confirm before publishing]

KeyReed also counts habits, such as British or American spelling, contractions, serial commas and short-message full stops, from text you hand to Fix or Improve and from samples you paste in. It keeps counts, never the text. The Style Card lists each habit with its evidence; Keep, Edit or Remove any of them, and anything you set yourself is never overridden. Quick Fix leaves alone habits you hold, and writing requests get a short style note. [confirm before publishing]

Pause, Reset, Export and Delete everything are in Settings; one switch turns learning from your edits off. Learned data is a plain JSON file beside your history, never sent anywhere. [confirm before publishing]

Light on low-end PCs

Dictation stays as fast as it was.

A performance profile (Auto, Standard, Light) keeps KeyReed small on PCs with four or fewer logical processors or 8 GB of memory or less: the small grammar model, fewer threads, and no reading of browser windows. What you correct stays here

What you say, write and correct stays on this PC. KeyReed never logs keystrokes and never captures the screen. To learn your words it re-checks the text box it just typed into, for up to a minute, compares in memory and keeps only the corrected words. One switch turns that off. [confirm before publishing]

How Quick Fix uses the clipboard

To read your selection, KeyReed copies it through the Windows clipboard, so the copy can show in clipboard history or a clipboard manager. KeyReed saves what was on your clipboard first and puts it back afterwards, and stops with a message if it cannot put it back. [confirm before publishing]
